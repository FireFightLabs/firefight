## Design Context

### Users
Engineering teams at YC-stage Seed/Series A SaaS startups (10-50 engineers). On-call responders, SREs, engineering managers. Uses the product during incidents — high-stress moments where clarity matters more than flair. Also during calm post-mortem hours where the product should feel like a place worth spending time.

### Brand Personality
Trustworthy. Calm. Premium. Understated.

Three words: **confident**, **clinical**, **grown-up**.

The product handles your worst day. It should feel like the tool a serious operator reaches for — not a cheerful marketing site, not a noisy alert dashboard. Dependable.

### Aesthetic Direction
References: Linear, incident.io, Vercel, Resend, Cal.com. Confident minimalism with craft visible in the details — typography, spacing, a single well-placed accent.

Anti-references: PagerDuty (busy, dated), Atlassian-family tools (corporate, heavy), anything with gradients-on-dark "SaaS landing page" aesthetic, cheerful cartoon illustrations, emoji-heavy interfaces.

Theme: **light mode primary**. Reasoning — incident management during the day/normal work hours is far more common than 3am emergencies, and the target audience associates dark-themed tools with consumer apps (YouTube, Twitter) rather than serious operator tools. Dark mode is supported but not the showcase.

Existing palette:
- Background: `#F6F8FB` (very light blue-gray)
- Foreground: `#0A1E2E` (dark navy)
- Primary: `#0E8FA3` (teal/cyan)
- Cards: `#FFFFFF`
- Border: `#E1E7ED`

### Design Principles

1. **Restraint over decoration.** Empty space is a feature. Every element earns its place.
2. **Type does the work.** Hierarchy comes from weight and size, not color or ornament.
3. **One accent, used rarely.** Cyan appears on primary actions and nowhere else.
4. **Craft in the seams.** Borders, shadows, optical alignment — the details that make it feel expensive.
5. **Feels like software, not marketing.** No glowy hero moments, no gradient backgrounds, no animated illustrations on the login page.
