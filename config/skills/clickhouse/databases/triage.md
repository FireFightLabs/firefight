---
name: clickhouse_triage
when: Starting on anything wrong with a ClickHouse Cloud service, such as slow or failing queries, inserts falling behind or the service being unreachable, before knowing what kind of problem it is
tools: [resource_status, run_history, search_errors, query_metrics, search_logs, get_services_list, get_organizations, start_service, stop_service]
references: [best-practices/agent-connect-mcp.md, best-practices/agent-query-safety.md]
---
Every read here is safe to call, and starting or stopping a service is a change the person is asked about. `resource_status`, `search_logs`, `search_errors` and `query_metrics` take a service by its name on the resource map and read its system tables across every replica. ClickHouse's server must be switched on for each service in the ClickHouse Cloud console, under the service's Connect button and then MCP. A call refused for one service while others answer usually means it is off there, and only a person with that permission can switch it on.

1. When the person did not name the service, find it with `get_organizations` and `get_services_list`, or on the resource map.
2. Call `resource_status`. Its state says what ClickHouse is doing: running, idle or stopped when it scaled to zero, awaking while it starts again, degraded or failed when it is broken. A service waking from idle takes 10 to 20 seconds for its first query, and a timeout then is expected, so try once more before treating it as an outage. Note its replicas, memory per replica and whether it is read only. A service that was stopped stays stopped until it is started: propose `start_service`, which asks the person, and also wakes an idle service ahead of its first query.
3. Call `run_history` for the service. It is the last 30 days of the service's activity log, newest first: who started, stopped, scaled or upgraded it, changed its settings or put it in maintenance, and when, each start or maintenance with how long it took. A change just before the trouble is the first suspect. It needs a connection made with an API key, and says so otherwise.
4. When data may be lost or a restore is on the table, call `search_logs` with `stream` backup for the service's backups, newest first, each with its type, status, size and when it finished. Restoring one makes a new service in the ClickHouse Cloud console, which a person does.
5. Call `search_errors` over a range that starts before the trouble. The errors ClickHouse raised, grouped by name, say which kind of problem this is.
6. Stopping a service with `stop_service` makes every query to it fail until it is started, so propose it only when the service itself does harm or runs up cost while nobody should use it, such as a runaway job or a leaked key reading data, never to calm a slow service. Say what stops working and ask.
7. Then load the skill that fits: clickhouse_slow_queries when queries are slow or time out, clickhouse_failing_queries when they fail, clickhouse_resources for memory, CPU or replicas under strain, and clickhouse_inserts when inserts fail or data arrives late.
