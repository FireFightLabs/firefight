---
name: on_call_alert
when: An alert started this run and nobody asked, as the facts' on_call says, such as a page at 3am with nobody awake yet
tools: [search_alerts, get_incident, get_resource_map, search_similar]
---
1. Start from the facts: the alerts and their fields, the resources and services the incident touches, and when the clues say it started. Call `search_alerts` with `status` firing and `since` a few minutes before the start, to see what else fired with it. Several services failing together point at something they share.
2. Read what the alert points at, everything you can at once rather than one after another: the resource's errors and logs (search_errors, search_logs) from a little before the start, its deploys and the deploys of what it depends on (recent_deploys, `get_resource_map` for the neighbours), and its metrics for errors, latency and requests over the hour before (query_metrics). A fresh reading always beats what a memory or an earlier answer says.
3. Tie it to a change. A deploy, a config change or a flag minutes before the start is the first suspect, and changes_before ranks code changes against the clues. Try to rule each suspect out before you name it. When nothing changed, look at traffic, a dependency or the provider's own status. `search_similar` finds past incidents that broke the same way and what fixed them.
4. Page with evidence. When on_call says pages_on_call, find who is on call for what broke with the connected on-call tool's skill, loaded with use_skill, and give their email and the step that showed it in conclude's page. Name the person the schedule has on call now, never the next one. With no on-call tool connected, say so in gaps and leave page out.
5. Propose the fix someone can apply in one press, each step with its undo. When on_call lists an unattended rule for the resource and the evidence supports that change, write the step exactly as the rule's fix_step says, the same tool and resource, since Halon then makes the change on its own once a fresh reading shows the rule's metric above its threshold. A rule only allows a change, it is never a reason for one. Propose what the evidence supports, and leave a change no rule covers for a person.
6. Write the summary for someone who was just woken up: what is broken and for whom, since when, the cause with the evidence that shows it, and the one thing to do now.
