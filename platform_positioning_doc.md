# Platform Positioning

## Market Context

Current competitors such as Rootly and incident.io are enterprise-focused, closed source, and increasingly positioning around AI SRE capabilities.

Their strengths are:

- deep enterprise workflows
- broad feature coverage
- mature sales-driven packaging
- strong process support for larger organizations

Their weakness for smaller companies is often the same thing:

- too expensive
- too heavy
- too enterprise-shaped
- too process-opinionated for early-stage or growth-stage startups

This creates an opening for Firefight.

---

## Target Segment

Firefight should target smaller startups and engineering-led companies up to roughly 100 people.

These teams often want:

- strong incident coordination
- a fast Slack-native workflow
- enough structure to run incidents well
- low setup overhead
- pricing that feels reasonable
- tooling that is friendly to engineers, not only large ops organizations

They usually do not want:

- heavyweight enterprise process layers
- large procurement cycles
- enterprise-grade pricing for a relatively small team

---

## Positioning

Firefight should not position itself as merely a cheaper incident.io or Rootly.

That is not strong enough.

The better position is:

- a modern incident platform for startups and smaller engineering teams
- lightweight, fast, and developer-friendly
- transparent and trustworthy through open-source core
- differentiated by practical code-aware AI investigation

In practical terms:

- not enterprise incident management with a discount
- incident response built for smaller teams that still need to move fast and investigate deeply

---

## Strategic Angle

The strongest wedge is the combination of:

- lower price point
- modern UX
- open-source core
- strong engineering ergonomics
- practical AI-assisted incident debugging

This is more defensible than trying to match large competitors feature-for-feature.

Firefight should win on:

- speed to value
- simplicity
- usability
- developer trust
- cost accessibility
- code-aware incident investigation

---

## Product Story

Customers are not really buying "incident management software."

They are buying:

- fewer painful incidents
- faster coordination when incidents happen
- faster root-cause understanding
- a better incident workflow for small teams
- better learning after incidents

This means Firefight's story should combine:

- incident coordination
- lightweight process support
- code-aware debugging and investigation

That positioning is stronger than being just another Slack incident bot.

---

## Open Source Strategy

The strongest strategy is likely:

- open source the core incident platform
- keep the full Code Intelligence layer private

Why:

- open source helps with trust, adoption, transparency, and developer goodwill
- smaller startups often respond well to open tools and lower lock-in
- the code intelligence layer is a stronger long-term moat than the core workflow surfaces alone

The real moat is not only the parser. It is the full intelligence engine:

- cross-repo retrieval
- ranking quality
- context assembly
- confidence and coverage logic
- reasoning and fix guidance

So the likely best split is:

- public: core Firefight platform
- private: Code Intelligence engine

---

## Competitive Edge

Firefight's most credible edge is:

- incident management for startups that cannot justify enterprise pricing
- a more developer-native and less enterprise-heavy experience
- open-source trust and transparency
- proprietary code intelligence that helps teams debug faster

That is a more compelling strategy than trying to beat Rootly or incident.io on enterprise breadth.

---

## Pricing View

Initial thinking:

- `$99` for 10 users
- `$149` for 30 users
- `$299` for unlimited users

This is attractive, but likely too cheap if the product becomes polished and includes AI/code intelligence.

Risks of underpricing:

- support burden grows faster than revenue
- AI and indexing costs scale with usage
- unlimited usage can become expensive quickly
- very low pricing may make the product feel less credible for serious incident response

More sustainable alternatives:

- increase plan pricing moderately
- separate code intelligence as an add-on
- limit AI/code indexing usage on lower tiers
- avoid unlimited heavy usage on the cheapest plans

A healthier direction may be closer to:

- `$99 / $199 / $499`

or:

- affordable core plans
- separate paid code intelligence layer

---

## What To Avoid

To win this market, Firefight should avoid:

- trying to copy every enterprise feature from Rootly or incident.io
- overbuilding process-heavy workflows too early
- making the product feel bloated for smaller teams
- overpromising AI before code intelligence quality is truly strong

---

## Strategic Recommendation

Firefight should focus on becoming:

- the incident platform for smaller engineering organizations
- the product that is easiest to adopt and easiest to trust
- the product that combines strong coordination with practical code-aware investigation

The ideal strategic shape is:

- open-source core platform
- proprietary intelligence engine
- startup-friendly positioning
- pricing that is accessible, but not so low that it destroys margin or perceived value

---

## Bottom Line

Firefight does not need to beat enterprise competitors at enterprise breadth.

It needs to be clearly better for smaller engineering teams that want:

- lower cost
- lower complexity
- modern workflows
- strong developer experience
- useful AI investigation tied to actual code and recent changes

That is a credible and differentiated position.
